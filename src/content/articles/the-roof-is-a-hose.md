A New Mexico roof sheds a lot of water in a short window. Monsoon does not drizzle. It dumps. If the downspout is missing, kicked sideways, or dumped on the high side of the house, that dump lands in the same dirt the condensate line and the cooler bleed-off already found.

People look for a mysterious soil condition and walk past a four-inch pipe aimed at the corner that's cracking. Grade that slopes back to the stem wall does the same job between storms: it holds whatever the roof delivered.

This is not beneath the work. It is the work when the pattern is wet-corner, dry-corner. Extend the downspout. Put a real splash on it. Fix the grade so water leaves the foundation instead of sitting against it. Then wait a season and see what the doors do.

If you pier first, you still have a hose on the corner. The steel will be in the ground. The water will still be on the clay.
