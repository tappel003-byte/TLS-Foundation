A single visit is a snapshot. Doors on a hot day. A crack at the width it happens to be that Wednesday. Moisture in the dirt from last night's rain. You can learn a lot in an afternoon. You cannot learn the calendar.

The notes that matter in this stack — cooler season, condensate, monsoon, winter close-up — only exist because someone watched the house across months. If the story is "it does this in July and that in January," and nobody has seen January, you don't have that story yet. You have a guess that sounds like it.

Mark the crack. Take the elevations. Come back. Or get the homeowner's photos dated across a year. The second look is not delay for its own sake. It is the difference between a seasonal clay cycle and a one-way drop, and those two bids are not the same bid.

If it is moving now and moving fast, don't wait a year out of caution. If it has looked like this since they bought the place, don't pier it this week out of momentum. Time is a tool. Use it when the house is offering it.
