Not every crack is a job.

Paint in the gap, cobwebs, a clean edge that hasn't dropped dust, a homeowner who says it has looked like that since they bought the place in 2011 — that is a mark from a move that already happened. Active movement sheds. It opens. The door that was planed last year needs planing again. The crack you marked in May is wider in September, or it is a different shape.

A proposal that piers a quiet house because the drywall is cracked is selling steel to a finished event. A proposal that ignores a crack because "all houses crack" is missing the one that is still going. The distinction is not mystical. It is whether anything is changing.

Mark it. Photograph it. Come back after a season. If nothing moved, you learned something that a one-visit bid cannot learn. If it moved, you have a date and a direction instead of a feeling in the doorway.

The expensive mistake is treating every old crack like an emergency. The other one is treating an active crack like décor. The house will tell you which it is if you let time into the evaluation.
