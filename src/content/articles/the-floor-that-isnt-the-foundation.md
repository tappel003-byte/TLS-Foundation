Sheet vinyl in an older slab house, radiant heat in the floor, waves in the kitchen that weren't there last year. Someone already used the word settlement.

Vinyl moves on its own. Heat dries it, seams shrink, the sheet can ruck up in a pattern that looks like a slab problem if you only look at the floor covering. I am not a flooring guy. I will not diagnose vinyl. I will look at the slab and the doors and the cracks in the gypsum and whether the elevations agree with the waves.

If the doors are fine, the stem wall is quiet, and the only distress is in the sheet, that is a flooring call. If the elevations show the same corner high or low that the vinyl is telegraphing, then you have two things on the same floor and you still don't get to skip the vinyl question.

The mistake is letting a floor covering write the foundation bid. The other mistake is ignoring the floor because vinyl can mislead. Look at both. Send the sheet to someone who works on sheet. Keep the foundation question on the concrete and the soil.
