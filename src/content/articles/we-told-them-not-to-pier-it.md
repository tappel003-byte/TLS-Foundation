The cracks were real. The bids were real. Three of them, all steel, all ready to schedule.

The house wasn't doing what the bids said it was doing.

The crack at the window had paint in it and a story that went back to 2014. The door that stuck in July had been swinging free since October. Nobody had a survey. Nobody had asked what the crack did in January. The soil next to that wall was dry. The cooler drain was piped. The grade fell away from the house.

Piers would have gone in. They would have been pretty. They would have been a settlement answer to a house that had already finished moving, or that was only moving with the season and had already eased. That is not a moral failing by the other companies. It is what a one-visit bid does with a visible crack.

The honest line was: mark it, photograph it, fix the one downspout that was still dumping on the corner, and don't put steel in this week. If it opens again next summer and keeps a little more than it gives back, that is a different conversation. This visit was not that conversation.

Some people hear "don't repair it" as you didn't want the work. Some people hear it as the first true sentence they've gotten on the house. Those are the people who call back in a year with a calendar instead of a panic. The job, when there is one, is still there. It just isn't this Thursday.
