The piers have a warranty. The house is still moving.

Those two facts can both be true. A warranty on the shaft is a promise about the product — it won't rust through, it won't snap, it was installed to a spec. It is not a promise that the clay will stop swelling, that the leak is off, or that the tips are in a layer that can hold this house. Read the paper. A lot of them warrant the pier, not the floor, not the doors, not the crack.

If the movement is still seasonal, the water is still in the story. If the repaired line is still dropping, depth and mechanism are still in the story. Waving the warranty at either one does not close the case. It tells you what they were willing to stand behind, which may not be the thing you actually bought the job for.

Ask what happens if the house keeps moving and the steel is fine. If the answer is "the piers are warranted," you know what the document is for.
