The tree had been there longer than the addition. Roots in the strip along the west wall, canopy throwing shade, soil in that bed drier than the rest of the yard for years. Then the tree came out. Six months later the door on that wall wouldn't close.

Clay that has been dried by roots will take water back when the roots are gone. Landscape irrigation, monsoon, even just the end of the shade — the soil rehydrates and swells. The house does not settle into the hole the tree left. It gets pushed. That is heave from the other direction, and it shows up on a delay. People expect the ground to fall in. They do not expect the wall to come up after the stump is gone.

The crack pattern follows the old dripline more than it follows a footing failure. The calendar follows the first wet season after removal, not the day the tree came down.

Piers under that wall, right then, are a settlement answer to a moisture change you just caused. Let the soil find its new number, get water off that bed if you can, and measure whether the movement is still going before you pin the wall. Some of these stop once the clay has taken the water it was going to take. Some don't. The survey and the winter reading tell you which. The stump does not.
