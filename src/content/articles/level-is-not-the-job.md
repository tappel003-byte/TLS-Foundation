Houses in this state are not level. A lot of them were not level the day the concrete truck left. Floor covering, a long room, a garage that was added, a slab that was struck off by eye — you can spend a fortune chasing zero and still have a house that was never going to read zero.

The job, when there is a job, is to stop the movement that is hurting the house. Get the water off it. Get the load onto ground that will hold it, if that is actually what the soil needs. Then stop.

A proposal that promises a billiard-table floor is selling a feeling. Some lift is possible. Some isn't, not without breaking things that were willing to live with the old number. Ask what "success" means before the shafts go in. If success means the doors work and the crack doesn't keep opening, say that. If someone needs flat for a remodel, that is a different scope and a different expectation.

Repair is not a reset to 1994. It is the house not getting worse.
