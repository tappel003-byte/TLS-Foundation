The lot was leveled before the house was. Cut on the high side, fill on the low side, slab poured across both like they were the same ground.

They are not. The cut is sitting on what was already there. The fill is sitting on what someone brought in, at whatever compaction that day got. One end of the house can be quiet for thirty years. The other end is the fill, doing what under-compacted fill does when it finally sees water or just sees time.

A crack through the middle of the plan is often that boundary, not a mystery soil under the whole footprint. Pier the fill side past the fill. Leave the cut side alone if it isn't moving. Treating the pad as one profile because the grader made it look flat is the same mistake as treating an addition and an original as one slab.

Look at the old slope if you can still see it in the street or the neighbor's yard. The house remembers it even when the lot doesn't.
