The packet had foam in it. Expanding polyurethane, pumped under the slab, "voids filled," house stabilized. It was priced like a repair and written like a foundation job.

Foam fills a void. It can lift a slab that is sitting over a hole. It does not take a house load down to a bearing layer. It does not turn off a leak. It does not stop clay from swelling. If the soil under that foam is still wet, or still collapsing, or still the reason the floor moved, you have a cushion in a hole and the same problem underneath it.

There are houses where foam is the right tool — a known void, soil conditions that actually fit it, no pretending it is underpinning. There are houses where it got sold because it is fast and it sounds like you did something under the floor. Those are not the same houses.

If two of the three bids are foam and one is piers, you are not looking at three prices for one method. You are looking at two different jobs. Ask what the foam is being asked to do. If the answer is "fix the foundation," the tool got promoted. If the answer is "fill this void after we know why the void is there," that is a sentence foam can live in.

The house will not sort that out for you. The packet has to.
