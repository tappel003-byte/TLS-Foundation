Two estimates. Both say nine piers. One of them does not include the corner the machine can't reach.

That is not an apples-to-apples price. The low number is the open walls. The high number, if it is honest, is the open walls plus the corner that needs a different tool, a smaller rig, or a day's work the other bid never priced. Limited access is not a footnote. It is a line item, or it is missing.

If the movement is in that corner, the cheaper nine-pier job is nine piers on the wrong part of the house. You can pay it, get a straight row along the driveway, and still own the door that started the call.

When the bids come in, don't count shafts. Count whether the tight corner is on the page. If it isn't, you are not looking at two prices for the same repair. You are looking at a complete scope and a partial one that happens to use the same digit.
