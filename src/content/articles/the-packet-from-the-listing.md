Close is in eleven days. The folder already has an inspection, a remark about monitoring the cracks, and two foundation bids written from a walkthrough that lasted less than the showing.

That clock is not a soil condition. It is a transaction. It makes everyone hungry for a sentence that lets the deal move — "needs piers," "monitor," "we can do it before closing." None of those sentences is a diagnosis. They are dates wearing work boots.

A floor-level survey in that window is a map, not a repair bid and not a stamped order to do anything. It will tell you whether the floors and the cracks are telling the same story, and whether that story looks old or live. It will not get you to closing by itself. A pier bid written from photos so the deal can close is the other direction: a scope invented to fit a calendar.

If you are the buyer, the question is whether you are buying a finished move, a seasonal cycle, or an active drop. Eleven days may not be enough to know. Sometimes the honest move is more time or a holdback, not a crew between now and recording. If you are the seller, a cheap repair to quiet the inspector is how the next owner inherits the water you didn't shut off.

The listing packet is a stack of opinions under pressure. Read it that way. The house is not in a hurry. The contract is.
