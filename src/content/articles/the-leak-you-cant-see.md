The yard was dry. The cooler drain was piped. The grade looked fine. The house was still moving in one room.

Supply lines, waste lines, and radiant loops leak under slabs. They don't announce it. A sewer leak is worse because it can run for years at a volume that never shows as a puddle and never stops. The soil under that part of the floor gets wet on a schedule that has nothing to do with monsoon or a neighbor's hose. Expansive clay heaves. Collapsible soil drops. Either way the pattern is local — one bathroom, one hallway, one corner — and it doesn't follow the calendar the way a swamp cooler or a condensate line does.

If the door binds in January the same as it does in July, look under the house before you look at the sky. A moisture meter at the slab, a camera in the line, a utility that will pressure-test — those are cheap next to a pier layout. Piers through wet soil that is still being fed by a broken pipe are a repair sitting on an open tap.

Find the leak. Shut it off. Give the soil time to do whatever it is going to do once the water stops. Then measure. The sequence is the whole note. Skip the first step and the bid is guessing.
